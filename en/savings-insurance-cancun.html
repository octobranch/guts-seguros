A short answer

What is savings insurance?

Savings insurance combines protection benefits with an accumulation component for a goal, depending on the product. The contract defines contributions, term, costs, benefits and withdrawal conditions. It is not automatically equivalent to a bank account or a guarantee of any advertised return.

A guide to your decision

How to assess a long-term savings plan

Start by comparing your goal with the commitment you are taking on. Read any sales illustration alongside the product’s contractual terms.

  • Define a goal, timeframe and contribution you can sustain.
  • Ask for guaranteed values and estimates to be distinguished in writing.
  • Review what happens if you withdraw funds or terminate early.
  • Check the currency, costs and terms of included protection.

Frequently asked questions: Savings insurance

General answers to help you understand a proposal. Request an individual review for your own circumstances.

Discuss my situation
Does savings insurance guarantee a return?

Do not assume so. Some proposals distinguish guaranteed benefits from projected values. Review the contract and the illustration’s assumptions; this page does not publish rates or promise returns.

Can I withdraw my money whenever I want?

Access depends on the policy. Withdrawals or cancellation may have conditions, costs and effects on protection. Request the available value for the date when you might need the funds.

Will I recover all contributions if I cancel?

Not necessarily. The surrender value may differ from your contributions. Request an explanation of the costs and product value table before purchasing.

Is savings insurance the same as a retirement savings plan?

The names do not make them equivalent. A product must meet specific requirements for any particular tax treatment. We do not assume tax benefits or that every savings insurance policy is a retirement savings plan.
